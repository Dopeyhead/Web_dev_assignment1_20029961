Vocabulary

Learning vocabulary is an important part of learning any language. While understanding grammar helps you construct sentences, vocabulary is what allows you to communicate meaning. It is useful to start with common words and phrases that you are likely to encounter in everyday situations.
In this section we will cover some basic greetings, useful questions, everyday expressions and common nouns, verbs and adjectives. Each example will include the Japanese writing, its Hiragana pronunciation, Romaji and English translation. This should help you recognise the words regardless of which Japanese writing system is being used.

Basics, Greetings and Questions

One of the first things worth learning in any language is how to greet people and communicate basic information. Japanese has different greetings depending on the time of day, as well as several polite expressions that are useful when meeting people or asking for help.
Politeness is particularly important in Japanese, especially when speaking to people you do not know. Here are some of the most commonly used expressions:

Common Greetings

  • Japanese: こんにちは
    Hiragana: こんにちは
    Romaji: Konnichiwa
    English: Hello / Good afternoon.
  • Japanese: おはようございます
    Hiragana: おはようございます
    Romaji: Ohayou gozaimasu
    English: Good morning.
  • Japanese: こんばんは
    Hiragana: こんばんは
    Romaji: Konbanwa
    English: Good evening.
  • Japanese: はじめまして
    Hiragana: はじめまして
    Romaji: Hajimemashite
    English: Nice to meet you.
  • Japanese: ありがとうございます
    Hiragana: ありがとうございます
    Romaji: Arigatou gozaimasu
    English: Thank you.
  • Japanese: すみません
    Hiragana: すみません
    Romaji: Sumimasen
    English: Excuse me / I'm sorry.
    This can be used to get someone's attention or to apologise.

Useful Questions and Expressions

These phrases are particularly useful when trying to communicate with someone who speaks Japanese. They can help you ask questions, understand information or explain when you do not understand something.

  • Japanese: これは何ですか？
    Hiragana: これはなんですか？
    Romaji: Kore wa nan desu ka?
    English: What is this?
  • Japanese: 英語を話せますか？
    Hiragana: えいごをはなせますか？
    Romaji: Eigo o hanasemasu ka?
    English: Can you speak English?
  • Japanese: どこですか？
    Hiragana: どこですか？
    Romaji: Doko desu ka?
    English: Where is it?
  • Japanese: いくらですか？
    Hiragana: いくらですか？
    Romaji: Ikura desu ka?
    English: How much is it?
  • Japanese: 分かりません
    Hiragana: わかりません
    Romaji: Wakarimasen
    English: I don't understand.

Daily Life, Food and Travel

Once you have learned some basic greetings and questions, it is useful to learn vocabulary that you might encounter in everyday situations. Whether you are visiting a restaurant, shopping or travelling around Japan, knowing a few common expressions can make communicating much easier.

Food and Restaurants

Japanese food is well known around the world, and if you ever visit Japan, knowing how to order food or ask for something in a restaurant can be very useful. Here are some expressions that you might hear or use when eating out:

  • Japanese: おすすめは何ですか？
    Hiragana: おすすめはなんですか？
    Romaji: Osusume wa nan desu ka?
    English: What do you recommend?
  • Japanese: これをお願いします
    Hiragana: これをおねがいします
    Romaji: Kore o onegaishimasu
    English: This one, please.
  • Japanese: 水をお願いします
    Hiragana: みずをおねがいします
    Romaji: Mizu o onegaishimasu
    English: Water, please.
  • Japanese: おいしい
    Hiragana: おいしい
    Romaji: Oishii
    English: Delicious.
  • Japanese: お会計をお願いします
    Hiragana: おかいけいをおねがいします
    Romaji: Okaikei o onegaishimasu
    English: The bill, please.

Shopping and Travel

When travelling around Japan, you will often need to ask for directions, find places or make purchases. These expressions can help you navigate shops, train stations and other public places.

  • Japanese: これはいくらですか？
    Hiragana: これはいくらですか？
    Romaji: Kore wa ikura desu ka?
    English: How much is this?
  • Japanese: カードは使えますか？
    Hiragana: かーどはつかえますか？
    Romaji: Kaado wa tsukaemasu ka?
    English: Can I use a card?
  • Japanese: 駅はどこですか？
    Hiragana: えきはどこですか？
    Romaji: Eki wa doko desu ka?
    English: Where is the station?
  • Japanese: トイレはどこですか？
    Hiragana: といれはどこですか？
    Romaji: Toire wa doko desu ka?
    English: Where is the toilet?
  • Japanese: 助けてください
    Hiragana: たすけてください
    Romaji: Tasukete kudasai
    English: Please help me.

Common Nouns, Verbs and Adjectives

Learning individual words is just as important as learning complete phrases. Understanding common nouns, verbs and adjectives allows you to recognise patterns in Japanese sentences and eventually create your own.
Unlike the previous sections, which focused on complete expressions, this section looks at individual words and how they are used.

Common Nouns

Nouns are words used to describe people, places, objects and things. Many Japanese nouns are written using Kanji, although they can also be written in Hiragana. For example, 駅 (Eki) means station and is used in the question 駅はどこですか (Eki wa doko desu ka?), meaning "Where is the station?"

  • Japanese: 人
    Hiragana: ひと
    Romaji: Hito
    English: Person.
  • Japanese: 名前
    Hiragana: なまえ
    Romaji: Namae
    English: Name.
  • Japanese: 駅
    Hiragana: えき
    Romaji: Eki
    English: Station.
  • Japanese: 水
    Hiragana: みず
    Romaji: Mizu
    English: Water.
  • Japanese: 食べ物
    Hiragana: たべもの
    Romaji: Tabemono
    English: Food.
  • Japanese: お金
    Hiragana: おかね
    Romaji: Okane
    English: Money.

Common Verbs

Verbs are used to describe actions, such as eating, drinking, going somewhere or speaking. Japanese verbs often appear towards the end of a sentence.
Japanese verbs also change depending on how formally you are speaking. For example, 食べる (Taberu) is the casual form of "to eat", while 食べます (Tabemasu) is its polite form.

  • Japanese: 食べる
    Hiragana: たべる
    Romaji: Taberu
    English: To eat.
    Polite form: 食べます (たべます, Tabemasu).
  • Japanese: 飲む
    Hiragana: のむ
    Romaji: Nomu
    English: To drink.
    Example: 水を飲みます (みずをのみます, Mizu o nomimasu) – I drink water.
  • Japanese: 行く
    Hiragana: いく
    Romaji: Iku
    English: To go.
    Example: 駅に行きます (えきにいきます, Eki ni ikimasu) – I go to the station.
  • Japanese: 話す
    Hiragana: はなす
    Romaji: Hanasu
    English: To speak.
    Example: 日本語を話します (にほんごをはなします, Nihongo o hanashimasu) – I speak Japanese.
  • Japanese: 買う
    Hiragana: かう
    Romaji: Kau
    English: To buy.
    Example: これを買います (これをかいます, Kore o kaimasu) – I will buy this.

Common Adjectives

Adjectives are used to describe things, such as their appearance, size, price or quality. They are particularly useful when talking about food, places and objects.
Japanese adjectives can also be used to form simple sentences. The following are some common examples:

  • Japanese: おいしい
    Hiragana: おいしい
    Romaji: Oishii
    English: Delicious.
  • Japanese: 大きい
    Hiragana: おおきい
    Romaji: Ookii
    English: Big / Large.
  • Japanese: 小さい
    Hiragana: ちいさい
    Romaji: Chiisai
    English: Small.
  • Japanese: 高い
    Hiragana: たかい
    Romaji: Takai
    English: Expensive / Tall.
  • Japanese: 安い
    Hiragana: やすい
    Romaji: Yasui
    English: Cheap / Inexpensive.
  • Japanese: 楽しい
    Hiragana: たのしい
    Romaji: Tanoshii
    English: Fun / Enjoyable.

Putting Vocabulary into Sentences

Knowing individual words is useful, but the real benefit comes from understanding how they fit together to form sentences. Japanese sentences generally follow a Subject-Object-Verb structure, although the subject is often left out when it is already understood from the context.
The following examples combine some of the vocabulary covered throughout this lesson.

  • Japanese: 水を飲みます。
    Hiragana: みずをのみます。
    Romaji: Mizu o nomimasu.
    English: I drink water.
  • Japanese: 駅に行きます。
    Hiragana: えきにいきます。
    Romaji: Eki ni ikimasu.
    English: I go to the station.
  • Japanese: この食べ物はおいしいです。
    Hiragana: このたべものはおいしいです。
    Romaji: Kono tabemono wa oishii desu.
    English: This food is delicious.
  • Japanese: この店は大きいです。
    Hiragana: このみせはおおきいです。
    Romaji: Kono mise wa ookii desu.
    English: This shop is big.

In these examples we can see the nouns, verbs and adjectives from above can be combined to created sentences. While particles and grammar are important to know when forming these sentences, you can actually manage to communicate in Japanese as long as you keep the verbs at the end of sentences. This is particly because grammar is not as strict in Japanese as it is in English.
Learning vocab will give you the biggest boost to understanding when others are speaking Japanese.
